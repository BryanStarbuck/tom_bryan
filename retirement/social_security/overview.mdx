# Social Security — Getting Proof of Monthly Income

**The goal:** a PDF that senior-apartment landlords will accept as proof of how much
Tom receives from Social Security every month.

**The document you want is called a "Benefit Verification Letter."**

That is the official SSA name. Landlords, lenders, and housing programs also call it:

* Budget letter
* Benefits letter
* Proof of income letter
* Proof of award letter
* Award letter

They are all the **same document**. If an apartment asks for any of those names, this
is what to send.

**What it contains:** Tom's name, date of birth, the type of benefit he receives, and
the **exact monthly benefit amount** — plus built-in identifiers SSA adds to prevent
forgery. This is exactly what housing applications need.

> Do **not** confuse this with the *Social Security Statement* (the earnings-history
> estimate for people not yet collecting) or the *SSA-1099* (the annual tax form).
> Those are different documents. The **Benefit Verification Letter** is the one that
> proves the current monthly payment.

---

## 1. The fastest path — online, instant PDF (recommended)

This is free, takes about 10 minutes to set up, and produces a **downloadable /
printable PDF immediately**. Available 24/7 in English and Spanish.

### Step A — Create a "my Social Security" account

| Purpose | URL |
|---|---|
| **Sign in / create account (start here)** | **https://www.ssa.gov/myaccount/** |
| Create account page directly | https://www.ssa.gov/myaccount/create.html |
| Account FAQs & help | https://www.ssa.gov/myaccount/account-faqs-and-help.html |

Tom does **not** need any pre-existing login. Anyone receiving benefits can create one.

Since 2026, SSA no longer runs its own username/password. You must sign in through one
of two federal credential providers:

* **Login.gov** — the default SSA points to first. Use this one.
* **ID.me** — the alternative, if Login.gov fails.

Identity proofing asks for: a **photo ID** (driver's license or state ID), a **selfie
photo**, his **Social Security number**, **date of birth**, and a **phone or address
verification** step. Expect 5–30 minutes the first time.

**What Tom needs on hand before starting:**

* Social Security number
* Washington driver's license (or California ID) — must **not** be expired
* Date of birth: 1945-10-15
* An email address he can check
* A cell phone that can receive a text security code
* The mailing address SSA currently has on file for him

### Step B — Download the letter

Once signed in:

1. Sign in at **https://www.ssa.gov/myaccount/**
2. Select the blue **"Replacement Documents"** link on the **right side** of the screen
3. Select **"Get a Benefit Verification Letter"**
4. Customize the letter (you can choose which details to include — for a landlord,
   include the **monthly benefit amount**)
5. Select **"Apply to Letter"**
6. Select **"Save a Copy"** to download the PDF (or **"Print Now"** to print)
7. Close the pop-up when done

That's it — instant PDF, no waiting, no mail.

*Source: SSA Publication No. 05-10673, "How to Get a Benefit Verification Letter."*

## 2. If he can't or won't do the online account — by phone

SSA will **mail** a paper letter. No PDF, and it takes time.

| Line | Number | Hours |
|---|---|---|
| **SSA national number** | **1-800-772-1213** | Live agents Mon–Fri, 8:00 a.m. – 7:00 p.m. local time |
| Automated service (same number) | 1-800-772-1213 | **24/7**, English & Spanish |
| TTY | 1-800-325-0778 | Same hours |
| **Local office (Hemet, CA — serves San Jacinto)** | **1-855-207-7083** | Mon–Fri, 9:00 a.m. – 4:00 p.m. |

**Automated shortcut:** call 1-800-772-1213 and when prompted, say **"Proof of Income."**
The automated system handles it without waiting for an agent. It runs a short (~4 minute)
identity check asking for **name, Social Security number, and date of birth**.

**Delivery:** SSA mails the letter within **10 business days** to the address they have
on file. It goes to the address on file — **not** to any address given on the call.
See the address warning in section 4.

**Social Security Administration**
3095 W Devonshire Ave, Hemet, CA 92545
*One-half block east of Sanderson (Hwy 79), one block north of Florida Ave (Hwy 74)*

* Hours: Mon–Fri, 9:00 a.m. – 4:00 p.m. Closed weekends and federal holidays.
* Local phone: 1-855-207-7083
* Office locator (if he moves to Redmond, WA): **https://www.ssa.gov/locator/**

**Appointments:** Since January 6, 2025, SSA field offices run on an **appointment-based
system**. Call **1-800-772-1213** (7 a.m. – 7 p.m., Mon–Fri) to book. There is **no
online appointment scheduling** as of 2026.

**Walk-ins:** SSA has stated it will **not turn people away** who cannot or do not want
to make an appointment — but expect a longer wait (roughly 26 minutes for walk-ins vs.
6 minutes with an appointment).

An office visit can also **complete the identity proofing** that unlocks the online
account, which is worth doing once so every future letter is a same-day download.

## 4. Two things that could go wrong for Tom specifically

### The address mismatch

Tom holds a **Washington driver's license listing 8300 160th Ave NE, Apt 303, Redmond,
WA 98052**, while he currently lives and receives mail at **San Jacinto, CA 92583**.

Online identity proofing cross-checks the ID against address records. A mismatch is one
of the most common reasons Login.gov / ID.me verification fails.

* If verification fails online, use **ID.me** instead of Login.gov, or fall back to
  in-person proofing.
* Any letter SSA **mails** goes to the address SSA has on file. Confirm which address
  that is *before* requesting a mailed copy — otherwise it lands at the wrong place.
* Once he moves to Washington, update the address with SSA (can be done inside the
  my Social Security account).

### Login.gov in-person option at the Post Office

If Login.gov can't verify him online, it offers **in-person verification at a Post
Office** — over 18,000 participating locations.

1. Start the process online at Login.gov
2. Choose the in-person option
3. Login.gov emails an **enrollment barcode** plus the 10 nearest participating post offices
4. Bring the barcode and a **non-expired** state-issued photo ID to the Post Office
5. He has **30 days** to complete it

**Login.gov help line: 1-844-875-6446 (TTY 711) — open 24/7.**

## 5. Other Social Security documents worth grabbing while logged in

Once the account exists, these are all downloadable from the same portal:

| Document | What it is | Useful for |
|---|---|---|
| **Benefit Verification Letter** | Current monthly benefit amount | **Apartment applications** ← the one we need |
| **SSA-1099** | Annual tax form showing total benefits paid | Taxes; sometimes accepted as income proof |
| **COLA notice** | Annual cost-of-living increase notice, posted each December | Shows the upcoming year's payment |
| **Payment history** | Record of deposits | Backup proof of income |

Note: Tom's Social Security is **not** reduced by his LACERA or CalPERS pensions — the
Windfall Elimination Provision was repealed by the Social Security Fairness Act (signed
January 2025).
