# Income Requirements — Brandenwood Senior Apartments

**This is the make-or-break page for Tom.**

## How Brandenwood's Income Rules Work

Brandenwood is financed through the **Washington State Housing Finance Commission's
Low-Income Housing Tax Credit (LIHTC) program**. That means income limits are
**maximums** — you must earn *at or below* the cap to qualify. This is the opposite of
a market-rate building, where a landlord checks that you earn *enough* (e.g., 2.5–3x rent).

Units are restricted at two tiers of King County Area Median Income (AMI):

* **45% AMI units** — lower rents, lower income cap
* **60% AMI units** — higher rents, higher income cap

## The Numbers vs. Tom's $65,000 Income

Approximate 2025 limits for the Seattle-Bellevue HUD metro area, one-person household:

| Tier | Income cap (1 person) | Tom at $65,000 |
|---|---|---|
| 45% AMI | ~$49,500 | **Over — does not qualify** |
| 60% AMI | ~$66,000 | **Under by ~$1,000 — qualifies, barely** |

## Why the Margin Is Risky

* **Countable income is broader than salary.** LIHTC certification counts anticipated
  gross income: wages, Social Security, pensions, plus *imputed income from assets*
  (interest, dividends, and asset value calculations). If Tom's total countable income
  crosses ~$66,000, he is disqualified.
* **Limits change every year.** HUD republishes AMI limits annually (usually April–June).
  They have generally been rising in King County, which helps, but the current year's
  number must be confirmed with the property.
* **Recertification.** Tax-credit properties recertify income; rules are more forgiving
  after move-in (the 140% rule), so the critical test is at initial application.

## Other Application Requirements

* Age 62+
* $375 security deposit; $25 per adult screening fee
* Section 8 vouchers accepted (not relevant to Tom, but shows the property type)
* Typical wait: 1–3 months

2. Exactly what income and assets do you count toward the limit?
3. How many 60% units are there vs. 45% units, and which have openings?
4. If income is within ~$1,000 of the cap, do applicants commonly get denied at
   verification?
5. What rent would apply at the 60% tier for a one-bedroom today?
